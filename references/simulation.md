# Independent synthetic runner test

Use only for an authorized validation task. Keep generated artifacts isolated from real athlete records. A persona is not a subject in a training study.

1. Give a separate agent only a role: invent a stable recreational-runner profile and make a short natural request. It should privately retain goals, training, schedule, recent race context and a plausible disruption, revealing them when asked. Do not provide the original user's profile or a desired outcome.
2. Conduct the real intake using this skill. Record questions and answers without retroactively improving the transcript. Use synthetic dates and state assumptions explicitly.
3. Give the persona a phase sketch and representative week or full plan to the requested horizon. Ask it to compare with its private constraints, identify confusion, excessive burden and missing options, then introduce its disruption. Do not ask it to fabricate injury, adaptation or finishing-time outcomes.
4. Revise the response and ask for a direct recheck. Reveal the private profile only after the intake and initial deliverable to expose omissions.
5. If useful and authorized, use an independent evaluator that sees only the skill, request and raw transcript. Report observable successes, actual failures, changes and remaining limits. Do not equate persona satisfaction or passing mechanical checks with coaching efficacy.

Keep scenarios varied across later runs: novice without races, experienced runner with divergent distances, hills/trails, shift work, little history, ambiguous units, uncertain event entry. Do not fit the universal workflow to a single persona.
